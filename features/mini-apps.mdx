When your agent has something for you to review, approve, or interact with, it doesn't just send a wall of text — it sends a mini-app. Mini-apps are lightweight interactive webviews that your agent delivers as iMessage app cards. You tap the card, make your change, and the card updates in place. No browser switch, no separate app, no second message asking if you saw the first one.

## What mini-apps look like in practice

Your agent sends a card into your iMessage thread. The card has a title and a short description of what's inside. You tap it, the webview opens inline, you take an action, and you're done. The card reflects the updated state immediately — it doesn't disappear and get replaced with a new message.

On the **web app**, the same interaction produces an inline panel inside the chat window rather than an iMessage card. Your agent's underlying code doesn't change — only the surface that renders it. Every mini-app works identically in both environments.

## How a mini-app interaction works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your agent sends a card">
    When your agent has something for you to act on, it sends an iMessage app card into your conversation. The card shows a summary of what's inside.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap to open">
    Tap the card to open the mini-app webview inline within Messages. The full interactive interface loads without leaving the app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take action">
    Move a Kanban card, check off a task, or watch your agent's desktop stream. The interface responds immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The card updates in place">
    Once you act, the card reflects the new state right in your message thread. No follow-up message, no confirmation prompt, no context switch.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Security

Each mini-app card carries a **single-use signed token** that expires within minutes of being generated. The token is scoped to that specific card and action — it can't be used to access anything else in your account.

If you forward a card to someone else, it will be non-functional: either already used or expired. Your data can't leak through a forwarded card, and a recipient can't act on your behalf by tapping a stale link.

<Info>
  Tokens are verified server-side on every action. Even if someone intercepts a card URL, they cannot perform actions after the token has been consumed or has expired.
</Info>

## Sending a mini-app manually

Your agent decides when to send a card based on context, but you can also push one yourself. In the web app, navigate to the relevant section — your Kanban board, To-Do list, and so on — and use the **Send to iMessage** button to push a fresh, live card directly into your Messages thread.
